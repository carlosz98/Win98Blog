e) Entity-Relationship Diagram (ERD)

The database has 9 MySQL tables. The users table is the center: everything on the site is saved with the user who created it. The files themselves (music, videos, podcast audio, photos) are stored in Google Cloud Storage, and the tables keep their links.

  • One-to-many: one user writes many articles and comments, and uploads many songs, videos and podcast episodes.

  • One-to-many: one category has many articles, and one article has many comments.

  • Many-to-many: a playlist has many songs, and a song can be in many playlists. The playlist_songs table connects them, one row per song in a playlist.

  • The role column in users decides what each person can do (admin, host, contributor or listener).

  • The status column in songs and videos lets the admin approve uploads before they appear on the site.

Many-to-many in plain words

Think of students and classes: one student takes many classes, and one class has many students. You can't write all of a student's classes in one box, so you keep a separate list with one line per pair:

StudentClass
CarlosMAC 272
CarlosMAC 110
AnaMAC 272

That list is the junction table. In this project, playlist_songs does the same job for playlists and songs. (The SQL version, with a tested JOIN, is in Week 6, Day 1 under "Relationships between tables".)
