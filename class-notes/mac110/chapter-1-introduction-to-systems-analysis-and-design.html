Chapter 1: Introduction to Systems Analysis and Design

Phase 1, Systems Planning. Systems analysis and design is the process of building information systems that turn data into useful information, and the systems analyst is the IT team member who plans, develops and maintains them.

Five components of an information system (memorize these):

  1. Hardware: everything in the physical layer.

  2. Software: system software manages the hardware; application software supports daily business work.

  3. Data: the raw facts the system turns into information.

  4. Processes: the tasks users, managers and IT staff perform.

  5. People: users inside and outside the company (also called end users).

Types of business information systems: enterprise computing (company-wide, such as ERP), transaction processing (TP, high-volume daily transactions like order entry), business support (job-related information and decision support for all levels), knowledge management (expert systems with a knowledge base and inference rules), and user productivity (email, office apps, groupware).

Organization levels and their information needs: top managers do strategic plans (mission and goals); middle managers and knowledge workers give direction and resources; supervisors and team leaders oversee operations; operational employees do the daily work. Empowerment pushes responsibility down to all levels.

Analyst tools: modeling (a graphical picture of a concept or process), prototyping (an early working model), and CASE tools (software that supports development tasks).

The IT department handles application development, systems support and security, user support (help desk), database administration, network administration, web support and quality assurance.

A good analyst understands the business, thinks critically (compare, classify, evaluate, find cause and effect), and communicates well. Certifications help a career.

Five IT trends in this edition: agile methods, cloud computing, mobile devices, service orientation and social media.
