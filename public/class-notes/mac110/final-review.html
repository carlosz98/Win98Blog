Final review

The ten things most likely to be on a test, across the whole book:

  1. The five SDLC phases, in order, with each deliverable.

  2. The five components of an information system.

  3. The four kinds of feasibility (operational, technical, economic, schedule).

  4. Finding the critical path and slack; the (B + 4P + W) / 6 estimate.

  5. The four DFD symbols, the three process errors, and leveling and balancing.

  6. The three O-O ideas (encapsulation, polymorphism, inheritance) and the UML diagrams.

  7. Payback, ROI and NPV.

  8. ERD relationships, keys, and normalization to 3NF.

  9. The four changeover methods ranked by cost and risk.

  10. The four maintenance types and the backup types.

Pairs that are easy to mix up:

PairThe difference
JAD vs. RADJAD produces a requirements model; RAD produces the working system
Logical vs. physical modelWhat the system does vs. how it is built
Context diagram vs. Diagram 0One process showing scope vs. the major processes inside it
Decision table vs. decision treeThe same logic as a grid vs. as branches
Class vs. instanceThe category vs. one specific object in it
RFP vs. RFQAsks vendors for a solution vs. asks for a price on a known product
Primary vs. foreign keyIdentifies a record vs. links to another table's primary key
2NF vs. 3NFRemoves partial dependencies vs. removes transitive dependencies
Fat vs. thin clientLogic on the client vs. logic on the server
Online vs. batchProcess right away vs. process in scheduled groups
Cohesion vs. couplingWant high cohesion and loose coupling
Direct cutover vs. parallelCheapest and riskiest vs. most expensive and safest
Adaptive vs. perfectiveNew requirements vs. better efficiency
Differential vs. incremental backupChanges since the last full vs. files never backed up
