Chapter 2: Analyzing the Business Case

Phase 1, Systems Planning. Every project starts from a business case: the reason for the proposed system, its justification and its estimated financial impact.

Strategic planning flows from the company's purpose, vision and values to a mission statement, then to goals, objectives, daily operations and business results for stakeholders (anyone affected by the company's performance).

SWOT analysis looks at internal Strengths and Weaknesses and external Opportunities and Threats. It works for the whole company or for a single IT project.

Why projects start: improved service, better performance, more information, stronger controls, reduced cost. What drives them: internal factors (user requests, top management directives, existing systems, the IT department) and external factors (vendors, technology, customers, competitors, the economy, government).

Four feasibility tests (a classic test question):

FeasibilityQuestion it answers
OperationalWill the system be used and will it fit how people work?
TechnicalDoes the company have, or can it get, the hardware, software and skills?
EconomicDo the projected benefits outweigh the costs (tangible and intangible), including TCO?
ScheduleCan it be done in an acceptable time frame?

Tangible vs. intangible: tangible costs and benefits can be measured in dollars (new hardware, lower labor cost); intangible ones are real but hard to measure (better morale, customer goodwill).

The preliminary investigation, in six steps:

  1. Understand the problem or opportunity.

  2. Define the project scope (its boundaries) and constraints (requirements the system must meet).

  3. Perform fact-finding (org charts, interviews, document review, observation, surveys).

  4. Analyze project usability, cost, benefit and schedule data.

  5. Evaluate feasibility.

  6. Present results and recommendations to management in a report.

Fact-finding tools: a fishbone (Ishikawa) diagram shows the possible causes of a problem branching off a spine; a Pareto chart is a bar chart that ranks causes by frequency (the 80/20 rule); an XY (scatter) chart shows whether two variables are correlated.

Constraints can be present or future, internal or external, mandatory or desirable. Watch for project creep: scope that keeps growing after the project starts.

Likely test points

  • Name and define the four kinds of feasibility.

  • The six steps of a preliminary investigation, in order.

  • What SWOT stands for and which parts are internal vs. external.

  • Which tool fits which job: fishbone (causes), Pareto (ranking), XY chart (correlation).

  • The preliminary report must estimate time, staffing, costs, benefits and expected results for the next phase.
