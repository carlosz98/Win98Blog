Chapter 7: Development Strategies

Phase 2, Systems Analysis. The last analysis step decides how the system will be obtained: build it, buy it, or outsource it. The phase ends with the system requirements document and a presentation to management.

Software as a Service (SaaS) treats software as a service you rent, not a product you own. Cloud computing is its ultimate form. Web 2.0 is the collaborative, social generation of the web.

Traditional vs. web-based systems: traditional systems must fit specific hardware, software and legacy systems and use the Internet only as an add-on. Web-based systems treat the web as the platform (environments such as .NET and WebSphere), so they are more scalable, less tied to hardware, and easier to outsource.

Outsourcing terms: an application service provider (ASP) rents application software for a subscription fee; an Internet business service (IBS) provides web servers, hosting and IT support. Fees can be fixed, subscription, or transaction-based. Offshore outsourcing sends work to another country. A value-added reseller (VAR) sells and customizes packages.

Cost-benefit tools (TCO is the most important factor):

  • Payback analysis: how long until accumulated benefits equal accumulated costs (the payback period).

  • Return on investment (ROI): total return compared with total cost, as a percentage.

  • Net present value (NPV): costs and benefits adjusted for when they happen, using a discount rate. A positive NPV is good.

ROI = \frac{\text{total benefits} - \text{total costs}}{\text{total costs}}

Software acquisition, seven steps:

  1. Evaluate the information system requirements.

  2. Consider network and web-related issues.

  3. Identify potential vendors or outsourcing options.

  4. Evaluate the alternatives (references, user groups, benchmarks).

  5. Perform cost-benefit analysis.

  6. Prepare a recommendation.

  7. Implement the solution.

RFP vs. RFQ: a request for proposal lists requirements and invites vendors to propose a solution; a request for quotation asks for a price on a specific product you already chose.

After the presentation management can: develop in-house, modify the current system, buy or customize a package, do more analysis, or stop the project.

Likely test points

  • Build vs. buy vs. outsource: pros and cons of each.

  • Payback, ROI and NPV: what each measures; compute a simple ROI or payback period.

  • RFP vs. RFQ.

  • ASP vs. IBS; the three outsourcing fee models.

  • The system requirements document is the deliverable of Phase 2.
